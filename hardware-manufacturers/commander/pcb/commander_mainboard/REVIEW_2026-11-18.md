# Mainboard DRC review, 2026-11-18 (overnight)

**Verdict: the mainboard cannot be ordered for a working prototype yet, and the remaining work is not something a
script can finish honestly.** Everything the DRC reported was classified below. What could be fixed without changing
the design was fixed; what is meant was turned into documented rules; the rest is listed with the reason and the way to
do it. The board as it stands would come back from JLCPCB without a working memory interface, eMMC, HDMI or Ethernet.

## 1. Fixed: signal vias shorted to the planes (the serious one)

110 clearance and 108 hole-clearance errors were **signal vias touching the GND (In1, In4) and 5 V (In3) planes at
0 mm**. The planes had been filled before those vias were added. Gerbers made from that file would have shorted the
vias to ground and to 5 V. Fixed by refilling every zone (with connectivity built, as KiCad's editor does); no track,
via or part moved. **Rule for every board: press B (refill zones) in KiCad, then run DRC, right before exporting
Gerbers.**

## 2. Made explicit: pin spacing inside fine-pitch chips (`commander_mainboard.kicad_dru`, new)

- **U9, RK817 PMIC, QFN-68 0.35 mm pitch**: 16 errors were 0.10 mm between its own neighbouring pads against the
  PWR_5V class's 0.15 mm. That is the package. Rule: between two pads of U9, 0.09 mm (JLCPCB's 6-layer minimum).
- **U30, TPS23754 PoE controller, HTSSOP-20**: 4 errors were 0.19 mm between its own pads against a 0.20 mm
  clearance set on the footprint. The 0.20 mm was meant for the surroundings (IPC-2221B asks 0.13 mm at 57 V). The
  override was moved from the footprint into the rules file: 0.20 mm from U30 to everything else, 0.18 mm between its
  own pins.

Nothing else is relaxed; every other rule is as before.

## 3. Fixed: three near-misses between parts

C324 moved 0.05 mm away from FB9 (was 0.115 mm), C332 0.012 mm away from C15 (was 0.141 mm; C342 on its other side
keeps more than its 0.10 mm), C150 0.05 mm away from the keyboard USB-C J20 (was 0.1485 mm); DRC passes all three now.
Their tracks still end on their pads (the unconnected count did not change).

Left, within JLCPCB's limits: EMMC_D1 track 0.098 mm from U10's pad N12 (rule 0.10; the eMMC area is redone anyway).

## 5. Parts that physically collide (23 of the 302 courtyard overlaps)

Courtyards are the part plus 0.25 mm; 279 overlaps are only those margins (no two pads of different parts closer than
0.15 mm, which JLCPCB's assembly accepts). These 23 overlap by more than 0.5 mm both ways, i.e. the parts touch:

- **eMMC U20 (BGA-153)**: Q5, Q8, C325, C320, R82, R151, C333, C152, C161, C21 inside its package outline. This is
  blocker 4 of the design notes (the eMMC sits over the PMIC and cannot be connected where it is); its place has to be
  found again, with its decoupling.
- **LPDDR4 U10**: C317 and C329 under the package (bottom face).
- **USB-C J20 (keyboard) and J2 (service)**: C151, C338, C331, C332 under J20's shell, C78 under J2's.
- **RK860 U3 (CPU regulator, WLCSP)**: C80, C79, R56, C82 inside its outline.
- **Mounting holes H1, H2**: C345 and C339 where the M2.5 screw head sits.

Each needs the part moved to a free spot and its connections re-routed. On a board this dense that is hand work in
KiCad (a scripted move would break the routing around it).

## 7. Warnings

64 dangling tracks / vias: ends of partial routes and fan-out vias waiting for the open links above (removing them
would throw away routing that the hand pass will use). 43 "isolated copper" items: sections of the supply pours on
In3 that will join their nets when the supply links of 6 are made. None of these is a fabrication fault.

## 7a. Checks after the changes

`build.py --keep-board`: ERC clean; model / schematic / board **consistent** (2150 pads, 0 differences); BOM and
placement files written. PoE isolation: `tools/check_head_isolation.py` (copper to copper, per layer, the check the
design notes use) **0 pairs under 2 mm** in the head; the older `tools/check_isolation.py` (pad boxes, all layers in
one) reports the same 514 pairs before and after tonight's changes - it counts the line-side nets (BS, TRn_48V), which
are meant to run next to each other, so it is not a new finding.

## 8. Fabrication class (checked against JLCPCB's published capabilities - confirm on their live page)

6 layers, 1.2 mm; smallest track 0.09 mm, smallest via 0.25 / 0.15 mm (ring 0.05 mm) placed in BGA pads: JLCPCB's
finest 6-layer class, with via-in-pad **epoxy filled and capped**; ask for their impedance-controlled 6-layer
stack-up and recompute the pair widths for it (USB 90 ohm, HDMI and Ethernet 100 ohm, LPDDR4 40 / 80 ohm): nothing on
this board has been checked against a real stack-up yet (design notes blocker 5). No fiducials (JLCPCB's assembly
does not require them).

## 9. The way to a prototype that works the first time

The bespoke core (RK3566 + LPDDR4 + eMMC + RK817 on our own board) is the part of the design with the highest risk
and the most work left, and the part where a first prototype is most likely to fail. Two ways to get hardware that
works the first time:

1. **Keep the core on a proven module for the first prototypes.** The repository already has this path: the strip
   test board (`../commander_strip_test`) carries everything new (PoE, the hubs, the bays, the battery port) and takes
   a stock **Radxa ZERO 3E** as the processor. It predates the hub split, the new bay receptacles and the bays'
   back-feed guard, so it needs the same updates the hub board got, and it has 249 open links of its own - but none
   of them is a memory bus. The same idea for the product: a compute module (Radxa CM3 / ZERO 3E class) on the
   mainboard instead of the bare SoC also fits the repairability goal.
2. **Have the core laid out by a layout engineer** from Rockchip's / Radxa's reference layout (blockers 1 and 5),
   then route the rest in KiCad's interactive router. Realistic for a later revision, not for a first-shot prototype.

What was not touched tonight: the routing itself (no scripted routing was added - more script output in this area
would only be ripped up by whoever lays out the core), the schematic, the hub board, the keyboard.
